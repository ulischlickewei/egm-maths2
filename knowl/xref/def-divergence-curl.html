Definition 4.3.8. Divergence and Curl.

Let \(D \subseteq \R^n\) and let \(\mathbf{v}\colon D \to \R^n\) be a differentiable vector field.
🔗
The divergence of \(\mathbf{v}\) is the scalar field
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{div}\mathbf{v} \colon D \to \R, \qquad \mathbf{x} \mapsto \frac{\partial v_1}{\partial x_1}(\mathbf{x}) + \frac{\partial v_2}{\partial x_2}(\mathbf{x}) + \cdots + \frac{\partial v_n}{\partial x_n}(\mathbf{x}). \end{equation*}
🔗
For \(n = 3\text{,}\) the curl of \(\mathbf{v}\) is the vector field
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{rot}\mathbf{v} \colon D \to \R^3, \qquad \mathbf{x} \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\partial v_3}{\partial x_2}(\mathbf{x}) - \dfrac{\partial v_2}{\partial x_3}(\mathbf{x}) \\[6pt] \dfrac{\partial v_1}{\partial x_3}(\mathbf{x}) - \dfrac{\partial v_3}{\partial x_1}(\mathbf{x}) \\[6pt] \dfrac{\partial v_2}{\partial x_1}(\mathbf{x}) - \dfrac{\partial v_1}{\partial x_2}(\mathbf{x}) \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
It is also written \(\nabla \times \mathbf{v}\text{.}\)
🔗
Physical interpretation. The divergence measures the net outward flux per unit volume at a point: a positive value indicates a source (fluid spreads outward), a negative value indicates a sink. The curl measures the circulation density: its magnitude gives the local rotation rate and its direction gives the axis of rotation.
