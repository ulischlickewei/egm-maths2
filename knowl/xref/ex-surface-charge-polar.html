Example 4.1.27. Surface Charge via Polar Coordinates.

A point electric charge \(q\) is located at height \(h\) above the origin of a grounded conducting plane (the \(xy\)-plane). The method of images replaces the grounded plane by a mirror charge chosen so that the plane has zero electric potential. For this setup, the induced surface charge density is
\begin{equation*} \sigma(x,y) = -\frac{hq}{2\pi}\cdot \frac{1}{\bigl(x^2+y^2+h^2\bigr)^{3/2}}. \end{equation*}
Compute the total induced charge on the disk of radius \(R\) centered at the origin.
🔗
Solution.
In polar coordinates the disk is \(0 \leq r \leq R\text{,}\) \(0 \leq \varphi \leq 2\pi\text{,}\) and \(x^2 + y^2 = r^2\text{.}\)
\begin{align*} q_D \amp= \iint_D \sigma\,dA = -\frac{hq}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R \frac{r}{\bigl(r^2+h^2\bigr)^{3/2}}\,dr\,d\varphi\\ \amp= -hq\int_0^R \frac{r}{\bigl(r^2+h^2\bigr)^{3/2}}\,dr. \end{align*}
Using the substitution \(u = r^2 + h^2\text{,}\) \(du = 2r\,dr\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} \int_0^R \frac{r\,dr}{(r^2+h^2)^{3/2}} \amp= \frac{1}{2}\int_{h^2}^{R^2+h^2} u^{-3/2}\,du = \frac{1}{2}\left[-\frac{2}{\sqrt{u}}\right]_{h^2}^{R^2+h^2}\\ \amp= \frac{1}{h} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{R^2+h^2}}. \end{align*}
Therefore
\begin{equation*} q_D = -hq\left(\frac{1}{h} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{R^2+h^2}}\right) = q\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + R^2/h^2}} - 1\right). \end{equation*}
