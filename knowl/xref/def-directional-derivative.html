Definition 2.1.44. Directional Derivative.

Let \(f\) be differentiable at \(\mathbf{x}_0\) and let \(\mathbf{r} = (r_1, \ldots, r_n)^\top\) be a unit vector (\(\|\mathbf{r}\| = 1\)).
🔗
  • The directional derivative of \(f\) at \(\mathbf{x}_0\) in direction \(\mathbf{r}\) is
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{r}}(\mathbf{x}_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(\mathbf{x}_0 + h\mathbf{r}) - f(\mathbf{x}_0)}{h}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  • For a differentiable function, the directional derivative equals the dot product of the gradient with \(\mathbf{r}\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{r}}(\mathbf{x}_0) = \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_0) \cdot \mathbf{r} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}(\mathbf{x}_0) r_1 + \cdots + \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n}(\mathbf{x}_0) r_n. \end{equation*}
    🔗
