Example 2.1.52. Second-Order Partial Derivatives.

Compute all second-order partial derivatives of \(f(x,y) = x^3 e^{2y}\text{.}\)
🔗
First-order partial derivatives:
\begin{equation*} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 3x^2 e^{2y}, \qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 2x^3 e^{2y}. \end{equation*}
Second-order partial derivatives:
\begin{align*} \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(3x^2 e^{2y}) = 6x e^{2y},\\ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y \, \partial x} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(3x^2 e^{2y}) = 6x^2 e^{2y},\\ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \, \partial y} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2x^3 e^{2y}) = 6x^2 e^{2y},\\ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(2x^3 e^{2y}) = 4x^3 e^{2y}. \end{align*}
Note that the two mixed partial derivatives are equal: \(\partial^2 f / \partial y\,\partial x = \partial^2 f / \partial x\,\partial y\text{.}\)
