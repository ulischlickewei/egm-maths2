Example 4.1.14. Normal Domain Representations.

  1. Express the disk of radius 2 centered at \((1,0)\) as a normal domain of Type I.
    🔗
    The boundary circle satisfies \((x-1)^2 + y^2 = 4\text{.}\) Solving for \(y\) gives the upper and lower boundary curves:
    \begin{equation*} y = \pm\sqrt{4-(x-1)^2}. \end{equation*}
    The \(x\)-extremes of the disk are \(x = -1\) and \(x = 3\text{.}\) Therefore the domain is:
    \begin{align*} -1 \amp\leq x \leq 3,\\ -\sqrt{4-(x-1)^2} \amp\leq y \leq \sqrt{4-(x-1)^2}. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Express the region \(A\) between the parabolas \(y = 2 - 3x^2\) and \(y = -x^2\) as a normal domain.
    🔗
    First find the intersection points:
    \begin{equation*} 2 - 3x^2 = -x^2 \;\Rightarrow\; 2 = 2x^2 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \pm 1. \end{equation*}
    For \(x \in [-1,1]\) the parabola \(y = 2-3x^2\) lies above \(y = -x^2\text{.}\) The normal domain is:
    \begin{align*} -1 \amp\leq x \leq 1,\\ -x^2 \amp\leq y \leq 2 - 3x^2. \end{align*}
